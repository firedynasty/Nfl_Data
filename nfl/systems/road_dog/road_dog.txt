① "Sun/Mon" — game must be Sunday or Monday.
Why: Thursday teams get ~3 days of rest instead of ~6, and in the data bad teams on short weeks covered only 45%. Sloppy, unpredictable games — the system sits them out. (No flag to override; it's hard-coded.)

② "away=underdog" — the points must go to the away team.
Why: home field is worth ~2–3 points free, so a small spread at home is a weak compliment. On the road it's a loud one. 26 years: road version ~60%, home version showed no edge. This week 1's rejected home dogs went 0–4. (--include-home loosens it — historically dilutes the edge.)

③ "+1-3.5" — the dog gets between 1 and 3.5 points.
Why: this is the secret detector. A 3–14 team "should" get ~6–7 on the road. Getting only +2.5 means Vegas's homework says they've improved. Getting +6.5 means the market agrees they're bad — no secret, no edge, and that bucket grades ~48% (a loser after the vig). (--min-pts / --max-pts to change the band.)

④ "prior<=0.294" — the dog's last-season win% must be ≤ 0.294 (≈ 5 wins in 17 games).
Why: the whole trick needs the public to think the team stinks, and the public only remembers last year's record. The win% comes from season_records() (line 58), which replays the previous season game-by-game counting wins. A team at 7–10 doesn't trigger enough public disgust — and good teams (like DEN at 14–3 getting +2.5) are a completely different bet. (--prior-max 0.354 would let 6–11 teams in — edge weakens but survives.)

⑤ "total<45" — the over/under must be below 45.
Why: points are worth more in low-scoring games. +3 in a 17–14 game is huge; +3 in a 38–35 game is a rounding error. This is the weakest of the five filters — the cell was 62% with it, 58.6% without it — so it adds a little, it isn't carrying the system. (--total-max 46 to loosen.)
